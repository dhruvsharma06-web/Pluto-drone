What an inspection looks for.

Four kinds of problem, in plain terms. Which of them the app detects reliably on the real drone is still being measured, and is reported under evidence below.

  • Wrong propeller type on a motor.

    A propeller made for the opposite spin direction is fitted to this motor. The app compares the type it sees with the type the verified reference lists for that motor.

  • Propellers swapped between motors.

    Each propeller is correct for some motor, just not the one it is on. Only checking every position against the verified configuration — not a generic quadcopter diagram — catches this.

  • Seated the wrong way up.

    Some installation errors change what the camera sees at the hub and blade edges. The app reports orientation only where the image actually shows it; pitch details a single photo cannot show are reported as unknown.

  • Evidence that is not there.

    Blur, poor light, a cropped arm, an unsupported angle or a propeller that cannot be told apart hide the details a check depends on. These never become a Pass.

How a check works.

Six steps, handled by separate parts of the app so that the camera, the vision core and the screen can each be tested and replaced on their own.

  1. Safety

    Disarm the drone and stop every motor.

    The app walks through the procedure and asks you to confirm it. A tick box cannot prove the hardware is disarmed, so the procedure comes first. The app never arms the drone or spins a motor.

  2. Acquisition

    Choose the live camera or upload a photo.

    Both inputs lead to the same check. The Android runtime prepares an upright, unmirrored image.

  3. Acquisition

    Capture evidence the check can use.

    The whole drone in frame, in focus, in usable light, from a supported view.

  4. Vision core

    Locate and classify each propeller.

    Find the motor positions, then read propeller type and any orientation detail the image actually shows.

  5. Vision core

    Compare with the verified reference.

    Every observation is checked against the supplied drone's verified configuration. No reference, no verdict.

  6. App screen

    Show the result and what to fix.

    One of three results, the affected motor highlighted, and a correction where the evidence supports one.

Three results. Nothing in between.

✓Pass
Every required check was observable and every propeller matched the verified reference. A Pass is a visual installation check, not general flight clearance.
✕Incorrect Installation
The evidence was sufficient and at least one propeller confirmably differs from the reference.
?Unable to Verify
No verified reference or engine, or the image could not support the check. Confirmed partial findings may still be listed — but never a Pass.

Loading and camera or device errors are separate screens, not results.

Built as separate parts, on purpose.

Detection and verification never depend on the screen or the phone. Each layer meets the next through a versioned contract, so the vision core can be reused on other platforms later. Android is the only target today; nothing else has been tested.

Known limits

  • Blur and poor light hide the details a check needs.
  • Occlusion and framing: a hidden or cropped arm leaves a motor unchecked.
  • Unsupported views: only the views listed in the verified reference count.
  • Uncertain classification is reported as unknown, not guessed.
  • Unobservable orientation: some pitch details cannot be seen in one image.
  • The reference itself: every verdict is only as right as the verified configuration.
