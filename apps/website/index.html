Taken apart.

Shell, arms, motors, propellers — every part the check depends on.

Right type. Right motor. Right way up.

Type
The propeller made for that motor.
Position
On the motor the reference names.
Orientation
Seated the right way up, where the image shows it.

A blade's resting angle is never an error. Anything one photo can't show ends in Unable to Verify.

Made to fly.

Checked on the ground first — every inspection happens disarmed, motors stopped.

How a check works.

  1. Disarm and confirmThe app never arms the drone or spins a motor.
  2. Photo or live cameraWhole drone in frame, in focus, in good light.
  3. Find each propellerPosition, type, and the orientation the image shows.
  4. Compare and guideAgainst the verified reference, then what to fix.

Three results.

Captured from the Android app on a real phone, running the on-device model. Hover a phone to focus it.

Pass

All four propellers found and matched to the reference.

“All four motors have the correct propellers installed. The rear left is counter-clockwise, the rear right is clockwise, the front left is clockwise, and the front right is counter-clockwise. You do not need to swap any propellers or retake the photo.”

Incorrect Installation

The exact motor to fix is marked, with the correct propeller type.

“The front left motor has a mismatch. It currently has a counter-clockwise propeller, but it needs a clockwise one. Remove the incorrect propeller from the front left motor and replace it with a clockwise propeller.”

Test photo edited to mirror one propeller, simulating a swap.

Unable to Verify

Incomplete, blurry or dark photos are refused, never guessed.

“The detector could not verify the propeller status because the drone view is incomplete. No specific propeller needs to be swapped yet. Retake the photo top-down, with the whole drone visible, the camera steady and good lighting.”
